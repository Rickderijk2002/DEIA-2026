# 2 Slide component library

The archetypes every slide is built from. **Brand-agnostic**: each entry says what the
component is for and how it is composed. What colour it wears comes from the brand.

Lifted out of the JADS brand profile on 2026-08-10. The JADS file keeps its own styling of
these components, which is the correct division: the archetype is deck physics, the
terracotta badge is JADS.

## Build every slide from an archetype

**Never invent a slide layout for a single slide.** A deck is recognisable because the same
seven shapes recur, and the audience learns them by the third slide.

| Archetype | Use |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Opening. Solid colour field, one headline, minimal metadata. The one slide allowed a full-bleed background |
| **Section divider** | A large number or keyword, one colour block, minimal copy. It exists to let the audience put the previous section down |
| **Standard content** | Heading top-left, two columns: narrative one side, visual the other |
| **Insight** | The headline states the conclusion, the evidence sits underneath, one annotation is highlighted |
| **Quote or big statement** | One large statement in a panel, short attribution or none |
| **Comparison** | Two matched columns of equal visual weight, unless one is the recommendation and is marked as such |
| **Closing** | Strong colour field, one closing statement, the call to action |

## The building blocks inside them

These are the components a content slide is assembled from. Each one exists because a
paragraph would have done the job worse.

**Page badge.** A small numbered tab pinned to a fixed corner on every slide except the
title. It replaces a footer page number, and it is the cheapest way to make a deck feel
built rather than typed.

**Eyebrow label.** One short line of small text directly above a headline, giving context
before the big statement. **Always paired with a headline, never alone.**

**Icon-divider list.** For a sequence of points, maximum four: a small mark, one short
sentence, a thin rule beneath separating it from the next. **Use this instead of a paragraph
whenever the content is a list of points rather than one narrative idea.**

**Numbered step card with connector.** For a two to four step process: equal-width cards,
each with a numbered badge, a title, a divider rule and one or two sentences. Adjacent cards
are joined by a short connector.

**Process-flow strip.** For an end-to-end pipeline, maximum five stages: equal boxes in one
row, a bold title and a small caption each, thin arrows between them. **One stage is
highlighted** to mark the point being argued about.

**Gap-analysis row.** One fixed-height panel per source: a thin accent bar, the topic name,
the citation, a bold "Gap:" label and its description. Closes with one bar stating in a
single sentence how the gaps are addressed.

**Key-differentiator callout.** A panel on an otherwise plain slide, a short bold label and
one or two sentences, with the most important phrase bold. **Once per deck**, near the end
of the argument.

**Component-breakdown pair.** When one statement decomposes into two or three parts: the
statement in a quote panel, then one card per component below it, each with a short label
and one sentence.

## Rhythm across the deck

**Most slides are the plain background with coloured components inside them.** Reserve a
full-colour field for the title, the closing, and at most one or two emphasis moments.

An alternating light and dark rhythm across a whole deck reads as a template rather than as
an argument, and it removes the one tool left for marking a genuinely important slide.

## Two rules that outrank the library

- **On any coloured panel, the heading is the brand's highest-contrast text colour**,
  effectively always white or near-white. An accent-coloured heading on a coloured fill is
  the most common contrast failure in a deck, and it looks deliberate, which is what makes
  it survive review.
- **Never draw an approximate icon.** Where no icon asset exists, use a deliberate geometric
  mark from the brand's shape language, consistently. It needs no apology and no caveat. If
  a real icon set arrives later, it replaces every mark at once.
